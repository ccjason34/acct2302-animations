Three Buckets of Cash

The statement of cash flows sorts every cash flow into operating, investing, or financing. Practice the bucket decision before building the full statement.

OperatingDay-to-day business
InvestingLong-term assets
FinancingOwners and lenders

Operating

Cash from normal operations: collecting from customers, paying employees, paying suppliers, and paying interest under U.S. GAAP.

Investing

Cash from buying or selling long-term assets, such as equipment, buildings, vehicles, or long-term investments.

Financing

Cash from owners and lenders: issuing stock, paying dividends, borrowing principal, or repaying loan principal.

Common Trap

Do not classify based only on whether cash went up or down. Classify based on why the cash moved: operations, long-term assets, or financing from owners and lenders.

Before Practice

Check The Idea In Plain Language

Terms & formulas
Why This Matters

The three buckets explain where cash came from and where it went.

Key Terms
  • Operating: Day-to-day business cash flows.
  • Investing: Long-term asset cash flows.
  • Financing: Owner and lender cash flows.
Takeaway

Ask what caused the cash flow, then choose the matching bucket.

Learning Objectives

  • Classify cash flows as operating, investing, or financing.
  • Explain what each cash-flow bucket represents.
  • Avoid classifying cash flows only by whether cash increased or decreased.

Indirect Method: Build Operating Cash

The textbook's indirect method starts with net income in the operating activities section, then converts accrual-basis income to cash from operations.

StartNet income $50,000
Noncash itemDepreciation $10,000
Investing gainGain $4,000
Final operating cash$60,000

1. Start with net income

Net income is accrual-basis profit, so it is the starting point, not the final cash number.

2. Adjust noncash and gains/losses

Add back noncash expenses. Add losses and subtract gains from investing or financing activities.

3. Adjust working capital

Current assets and current liabilities show timing differences between income and cash.

Add decrease in Inventory+$5,000
Add increase in Accounts Payable+$7,000
Net cash provided by operating activities$60,000

Why This Step?

The indirect method is a bridge from net income to cash from operations. Each step explains why income and cash are different.
AA / LL shortcutAssets Alternate: assets go opposite. Liabilities Like: liabilities go with the change.

Common Trap

Do not treat every increase as “good” or every decrease as “bad.” For current assets, an increase usually means cash is tied up, so subtract it. For current liabilities, an increase usually means cash has not been paid yet, so add it.

Real-World Case: Netflix — Profit vs. Cash

Netflix reported growing net income (roughly $187 million to $1.2 billion across 2016–2018) even as free cash flow sank to about −$3 billion — because it paid cash for content years before that cost became an expense. By 2023 the timing reversed and free cash flow turned strongly positive (about +$6.9 billion).

Profit on the statement

Net income looked healthy and rose every year.

Cash going out first

Huge upfront content payments drained cash long before the expense was recorded.

Timing difference

The cost is spread over years as amortization; the cash left immediately.

Cash-flow question: how can a company report profit while its operating cash flow is negative?

Check The Idea In Plain Language

Terms & formulas
Why This Matters

Income and cash differ; the indirect method fixes the timing.

Key Terms
  • Indirect method: Starts with net income and adjusts to cash from operations.
  • Noncash expense: Expense that reduced income but did not use cash.
  • Working capital: Current assets and current liabilities.
  • Gain: Subtracted if it belongs outside operating cash flow.
Takeaway

Ask whether each line made net income different from cash, then add or subtract to fix the timing difference.

Show The Math

$50,000 net income + $10,000 depreciation - $4,000 gain - $8,000 A/R (accounts receivable) + $5,000 inventory + $7,000 A/P (accounts payable) = $60,000 operating cash.

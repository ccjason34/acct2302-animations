Two Points, One Cost Formula 📈

Use the high and low activity points to split a mixed cost into fixed cost and variable rate.

High activity3,000 hrs, $8,000
Low activity1,000 hrs, $4,000
Variable rate$2 per oven hr
Fixed cost$2,000
Find the high and low points to begin.

🔮 Predict: which two months does the high-low method use?
High point: 3,000 hrs, $8,000  ·  Low point: 1,000 hrs, $4,000
Variable rate (b) = ($8,000 − $4,000) ÷ (3,000 − 1,000) = $4,000 ÷ 2,000 = $2 / oven hour
Fixed cost (a) = $8,000 − ($2 × 3,000) = $8,000 − $6,000 = $2,000
Cost = $2,000 + $2 × (oven hours)

⚠️ Common Trap

Pick the high and low by activity (the x-driver: oven hours), not by cost. And remember high-low uses only two of the data points and ignores the rest — it's fast, but a regression that uses every point is more accurate.

Check The Idea In Plain Language

Terms & formulas
Why This Matters

High-low gives a quick way to estimate a mixed-cost formula from two observed activity levels.

Key Terms
  • High-low method: Uses highest and lowest activity points to estimate cost behavior.
  • Variable rate: Change in cost divided by change in activity.
  • Fixed cost: Total cost minus variable portion.
  • Mixed cost: Cost with fixed and variable components.
Takeaway

Use the high and low activity points, not simply the highest and lowest costs.

Show The Math

Variable rate = (high cost - low cost) / (high activity - low activity). Fixed cost = total cost - variable rate x activity.

Learning Objectives

  • Select the correct high and low points (based on activity).
  • Calculate the variable rate = change in cost ÷ change in activity.
  • Solve for fixed cost and write the cost equation y = a + bx.

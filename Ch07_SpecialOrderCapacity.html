Special Order: Idle or Full?

A one-time customer offers a lower price. The answer depends on whether the company has idle capacity or must give up regular sales.

Special order1,000 units @ $18
Variable cost$14/unit
Regular price$25/unit
Assumption: the special order will not affect regular selling price or future customer expectations.
The decision rule will build automatically; use Next step to control it.
Step 1
Start With The Order
Look at the order on its own first. Each unit sells for $18 and adds $14 of cost to make it. $18 − $14 = $4 extra profit per unit
Step 2
Check Capacity
Ask whether the 1,000-unit order fits into unused capacity. If it fits, no regular sales are sacrificed.
Step 3
Charge Only The Sacrifice
Only displaced regular units get opportunity cost. A displaced regular unit gives up $25 − $14 = $11 contribution margin.

Common Trap

The special-order price can be below the regular price and still be acceptable with idle capacity. But at full capacity, opportunity cost can turn the same order into a bad deal.

Before Practice

Check The Idea In Plain Language

Terms & formulas
Why This Matters

The same special order can be profitable with idle capacity and unprofitable when regular sales are displaced.

Key Terms
  • Special order: A one-time order outside normal sales.
  • Idle capacity: Unused production ability.
  • Opportunity cost: Regular contribution margin lost at full capacity.
  • Incremental profit: Extra revenue minus extra costs.
Takeaway

Capacity decides whether opportunity cost enters the special-order decision.

Show The Math

Idle capacity: $18 - $14 = $4 profit per unit. Full capacity: $18 - $14 - $11 lost CM = $7 loss per unit.

Learning Objectives

  • Compare special-order price with incremental cost.
  • Include opportunity cost when capacity is full.
  • Explain why idle capacity can change the decision.

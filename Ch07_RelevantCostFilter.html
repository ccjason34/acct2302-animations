⚠️ Common Trap

Ignore sunk and unavoidable costs. Including costs that do not change can flip the decision.

Real-World Case: The Concorde and Sunk Cost

Britain and France kept funding the Concorde for years after it was clear the jet would never be commercially viable — over $2.8 billion spent and only 20 aircraft ever built — largely to avoid "wasting" the money already poured in. Economists named this mistake the "Concorde fallacy."

Already spent (sunk)

The development billions were gone whether the project continued or stopped, so they could not be recovered either way.

Actually relevant

Only the future costs and revenues of continuing should have driven the keep-or-stop decision.

The trap

Leaders pressed on to avoid admitting a loss — the sunk-cost mistake this filter is built to remove.

Relevant-cost question: should money already spent change whether you keep going on a project?

Check The Idea In Plain Language

Terms & formulas
Why This Matters

Relevant-cost thinking keeps decisions focused on costs and benefits that actually change.

Key Terms
  • Relevant cost: Future cost that differs across alternatives.
  • Sunk cost: Past cost that cannot be changed.
  • Avoidable cost: Cost that disappears if an option is chosen.
  • Opportunity cost: Benefit given up by choosing another option.
Takeaway

Ignore sunk costs. Keep future amounts only when they differ between choices.

Show The Math

Decision impact = future revenues that change - future costs that change - opportunity costs.

Learning Objectives

  • Classify costs as relevant (differ between options) or irrelevant (sunk or unavoidable).
  • Ignore sunk costs and allocated costs that continue either way.
  • Decide make-or-buy using only the relevant costs.

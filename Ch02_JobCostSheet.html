⚠️ Common Trap

The overhead on a job cost sheet is applied (predetermined rate × actual base) — it is not the actual overhead bill. Actual overhead isn't known until later. Also: unit cost is total job cost ÷ number of units, not the cost of the last item added.

Real-World Case: Why "Forrest Gump" Showed a Loss

A studio costs each film like a job. Forrest Gump took in about $678 million on a roughly $55 million budget, yet the studio's "net profit" books reported a loss — after a 15% studio-overhead charge and a 32%-of-gross distribution fee were piled onto the job.

The job

Each film gathers its own direct costs plus a share of studio overhead, just like a job cost sheet.

Overhead piled on

A 15% overhead charge and a 32% distribution fee were layered on top of the film's direct costs.

The result

A blockbuster that "lost" money on paper. A judge in a related case called the method "unconscionable."

Check The Idea In Plain Language

Terms & formulas
Why This Matters

Job costing shows how a custom order gets a cost from materials, labor, and applied overhead.

Key Terms
  • Job cost sheet: The record that accumulates cost for one job.
  • Direct materials: Materials traceable to the job.
  • Direct labor: Labor traceable to the job.
  • Applied overhead: Overhead assigned using a rate.
Takeaway

The job cost sheet is the receipt for the job: it tells what the job used and what it cost.

Show The Math

Total job cost = direct materials + direct labor + applied overhead. Applied overhead = overhead rate x actual activity.

Learning Objectives

  • Accumulate direct materials, direct labor, and applied overhead on a job cost sheet.
  • Apply manufacturing overhead using a predetermined rate × an allocation base.
  • Compute a job's total cost and its cost per unit.

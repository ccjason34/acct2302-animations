1. The Concorde fallacy

Best fit

Britain and France continued supporting Concorde even after the project looked commercially weak. The teaching point is exactly the relevant-cost filter: money already spent is sunk and should not drive the go/no-go decision.

Use with

Ch07 relevant-cost filter

Class question

Should past spending affect a future continue-or-stop decision?

Open Ch07 filter

2. Forrest Gump and Hollywood accounting

Strong

Each film can be treated like a job. Hollywood accounting makes a familiar point: overhead charges, distribution fees, and internal allocations can change whether a job appears profitable.

Use with

Job cost sheets; plantwide versus departmental overhead

Class question

Who benefits when overhead is allocated to a job?

3. Movie-theater popcorn

Best fit

Tickets bring people into the theater, but concessions carry much of the profit. Stanford GSB explains that concessions are a smaller share of revenue but a much larger share of profit because theaters keep the concession dollars.

Use with

Contribution margin; target profit; margin of safety

Class question

Where does the profit really come from: tickets or popcorn?

5. Walmart vs. LVMH DuPont analysis

Good with verification

Walmart and LVMH can reach returns through different levers: Walmart relies on high turnover and thin margins, while luxury businesses rely more on high margins and slower turnover.

Use with

ROI = margin x turnover

Class question

Can two companies earn similar ROI in completely different ways?

Open Ch11 ROI

6. Airlines selling the last empty seat

Best fit

Once a flight is scheduled, many costs are fixed or sunk. If a seat would otherwise fly empty, a low fare can still add contribution margin as long as incremental revenue exceeds incremental cost.

Use with

Fixed versus variable cost; special order with idle capacity

Class question

Why sell a seat cheaply at the last minute?

Open Ch05 cost behaviorOpen Ch07 special order

8. Amazon shareholder letter: cash flow over earnings

Best fit

Amazon's shareholder letter is a primary-source NPV teaching hook: value depends on future cash flows, not just accounting earnings. It is excellent for long-term investment decisions.

Use with

NPV; future cash flows; investment decisions

Class question

Why can earnings growth destroy value if cash flows are weak?

9. Netflix: profit versus cash burn

Best fit

Netflix is a strong cash-flow timing case: content cash can go out before the related expense fully appears in net income. That makes it useful for explaining why net income and operating cash flow differ.

Use with

Indirect operating cash flow

Class question

How can a company report profit but still consume cash?

10. John Carter and movie break-even

Good

A movie can need far more than its production budget in box-office revenue to break even because studios do not keep every ticket dollar and marketing costs matter.

⚠️ Common Trap

A plantwide rate works best when departments use overhead in step with the same base. Here they do not: Machining uses machine hours, while Assembly uses labor hours. Departmental rates match each department to the right base.

Before Practice

Check The Idea In Plain Language

Terms & formulas
Why This Matters

One factory-wide rate can hide which department actually drives overhead.

Key Terms
  • Plantwide rate: One overhead rate for the whole factory.
  • Departmental rate: A separate overhead rate for each department.
  • Activity base: The driver used to assign overhead.
  • Cost distortion: When a product gets too much or too little overhead.
Takeaway

More detailed rates help when products use departments differently.

Show The Math

Plantwide: $20 × 10 labor hours = $200. Departmental: ($20 × 80 machine hours) + ($5 × 10 labor hours) = $1,600 + $50 = $1,650. The plantwide rate counts only labor hours, so it under-charges this machining-heavy job by $1,450.

Learning Objectives

  • Compute a plantwide overhead rate and separate departmental rates.
  • Match each overhead rate to its activity base (machine hours vs. labor hours).
  • Explain why a single plantwide rate under-costs a job that is heavy in a high-overhead department.

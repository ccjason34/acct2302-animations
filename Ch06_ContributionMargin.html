⚠️ Common Trap

Contribution margin covers fixed costs first. Only sales beyond break-even create profit.

Real-World Case: Movie-Theater Popcorn

Theaters hand up to ~90% of opening-week ticket money to the studios, so tickets barely cover costs. Concessions are the opposite: a bag of popcorn costs the theater about $0.90 and sells for around $8 — roughly an 85% margin — and concessions deliver about 40% of profits from only ~20% of revenue.

Thin-margin product

Most ticket revenue goes to the studio, so each ticket's contribution margin is small.

High-margin product

Popcorn and soda keep almost all of their price as contribution margin.

The strategy

Tickets are a loss leader to fill seats; concessions carry the contribution that covers fixed costs.

Contribution-margin question: which product really carries the contribution margin — and why is the popcorn so expensive?

Check The Idea In Plain Language

Terms & formulas
Why This Matters

Contribution margin is the engine behind break-even, target profit, and margin of safety.

Key Terms
  • CM per unit: Selling price minus variable cost per unit.
  • CM ratio: Contribution margin divided by sales.
  • Target profit: Profit goal included in the CVP formula.
  • Margin of safety: Actual sales above break-even sales.
Takeaway

Contribution margin shows how much each sale helps cover fixed costs and profit.

Show The Math

Target-profit units = (fixed costs + target profit) / CM per unit. Margin of safety = actual sales - break-even sales.

Learning Objectives

  • Explain how contribution margin first covers fixed costs, then becomes profit.
  • Calculate units needed for a target profit.
  • Compute the margin of safety.

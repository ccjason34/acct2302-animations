⚠️ Common Trap

The report only balances if each step uses the right unit count. Assign cost to ending WIP with its equivalent units (1,200), never its physical units (3,000) — using 3,000 would push $18,000 into WIP and leave the totals out of balance. And under the weighted-average method, the cost per EU blends beginning-WIP cost with this period's cost; here beginning WIP is $0, so the two methods give the same answer.

Check The Idea In Plain Language

Terms & formulas
Why This Matters

The production cost report is how a process-costing department splits its total cost between units that left and units still in process — and proves nothing was lost.

Key Terms
  • Units to account for: Beginning WIP + units started.
  • Equivalent units: Completed units + partial work in ending WIP.
  • Cost per equivalent unit: Costs to account for ÷ equivalent units.
  • Cost reconciliation: Proof that costs assigned equal costs to account for.
Takeaway

Same five steps every time: units in = units out, convert to EU, cost the EU, assign the dollars, then check the total balances.

Show The Math

Units to account for = beginning WIP + started. Equivalent units = completed + (ending WIP × % complete). Cost per EU = costs to account for ÷ equivalent units. Cost to WIP = ending-WIP equivalent units × cost per EU. Reconcile: cost to completed + cost to WIP = costs to account for.

Learning Objectives

  • Reconcile physical units so units to account for equal units accounted for.
  • Convert physical units to equivalent units and compute cost per equivalent unit.
  • Assign costs to completed units and ending WIP, then prove the report balances.

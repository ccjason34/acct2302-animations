ABC Isn't Just for Factories 🏦

A community bank makes no product and holds no inventory — but it still has huge overhead. Watch how flat "cost per account" hides which customers are actually expensive to serve, and how ABC fixes it.

Traditional: cost per account

Spread all $500,000 over 10,000 accounts, so every account receives the same $50 service cost.

ABC: cost per service activity

Transactions$200,000 / 400,000 txns = $0.50 each

⚠️ Common Trap

ABC isn't only for factories — banks, hospitals, airlines, and universities are overhead-heavy service businesses where it shines. The cost driver is the activity a customer demands (calls, transactions, loan reviews), not a flat headcount. And as always, ABC doesn't change the total $500,000 — it just redistributes it to reveal who's really expensive to serve.

Check The Idea In Plain Language

Terms & formulas
Why This Matters

Service businesses also consume activities, even when there is no physical product.

Key Terms
  • Service activity: Work such as processing, customer calls, or applications.
  • Driver: The measurable cause of activity cost.
  • Cost object: The customer, service, or product receiving cost.
  • ABC rate: Activity cost divided by activity volume.
Takeaway

ABC can reveal which services or customers require the most support work.

Show The Math

Traditional: $50 per account. Basic = $400,000; Business = $100,000.

ABC rates: $0.50 per transaction, $1.50 per call, $10 per loan application. Basic = $250,000; Business = $250,000.

Learning Objectives

  • Apply activity-based costing to a service business.
  • Compute activity rates and assign overhead to customer segments.
  • Explain why a single broad base mis-costs high-activity customers.

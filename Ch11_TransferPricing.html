Transfer Price: Find the Acceptable Range

When one division sells to another inside the company, the transfer price must make sense for both the selling and buying divisions.

Test A Transfer Price

Common Trap

The seller minimum depends on capacity. Idle capacity usually lowers the minimum; full capacity adds opportunity cost from lost outside sales.

Real-World Case: Apple's €13B Transfer-Pricing Case

How Apple priced transactions among its own Irish subsidiaries let it report an effective tax rate as low as 0.005% (2014). In September 2024 the EU Court of Justice ordered Ireland to recover €13 billion — a case that turned on whether those internal, related-party prices were "arm's length."

Related-party prices

Units of the same company charged each other for rights, goods, and services.

The arm's-length test

Internal transfer prices are supposed to match what independent firms would charge.

Why it matters

The transfer price decides which division — and which country — reports the profit and pays the tax.

Transfer-pricing question: how should related divisions set the price for what they sell each other?

Before Practice

Check The Idea In Plain Language

Terms & formulas
Why This Matters

Transfer pricing protects both divisions from being forced into a bad internal trade.

Key Terms
  • Seller minimum: Lowest price the selling division should accept.
  • Buyer maximum: Highest price the buying division should pay.
  • Idle capacity: Unused capacity that lowers the seller minimum.
  • Internal range: Prices acceptable to both divisions.
Takeaway

A transfer works when seller minimum <= buyer maximum.

Show The Math

Idle capacity range: $18 seller minimum to $28 buyer maximum. Full capacity: seller minimum $30 > buyer maximum $28, so no range.

Learning Objectives

  • Identify the seller minimum transfer price.
  • Identify the buyer maximum transfer price.
  • Decide whether an internal transfer range exists.

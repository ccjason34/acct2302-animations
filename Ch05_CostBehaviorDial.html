⚠️ Common Trap

Total fixed cost stays flat, but fixed cost per unit falls as volume rises. Total variable cost rises with volume, but variable cost per unit stays constant.

Real-World Case: The Airline's Empty Seat

Once a flight is scheduled, almost all of its cost — crew, fuel, the aircraft, the gate — is fixed and already committed. The extra (variable) cost of one more passenger is tiny, which is why an airline will sell an otherwise-empty seat at a deep discount.

Large fixed cost

The flight costs nearly the same whether 100 or 150 people board.

Tiny variable cost

One more passenger adds only a little fuel, a snack, and some paperwork.

Cost per seat falls

Spreading the fixed cost over more passengers lowers the cost per seat.

Cost-behavior question: why can an airline profitably sell a last-minute seat for far less than a seat's average cost?

Source for discussion: GlobalAir, "The Hidden Economics of Empty Airline Seats". Used as a class discussion prompt; figures are approximate.

Check The Idea In Plain Language

Terms & formulas
Why This Matters

Cost behavior helps predict what total cost will do when activity rises or falls.

Key Terms
  • Variable cost: Changes in total with activity.
  • Fixed cost: Stays the same in total within the relevant range.
  • Mixed cost: Has both fixed and variable parts.
  • Relevant range: The activity band where the cost pattern holds.
Takeaway

Watch total cost and per-unit cost separately. They do not always move the same way.

Show The Math

Total cost = fixed cost + (variable cost per unit x units). Variable cost per unit stays constant; fixed cost per unit changes as volume changes.

Learning Objectives

  • Distinguish variable, fixed, and mixed costs by how they react to volume.
  • Predict what happens to total cost vs. per-unit cost as activity changes.
  • Read a cost-behavior graph.
